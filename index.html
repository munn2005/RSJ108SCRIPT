How to write a script

Time needed: As much time you have before your deadline
Awesome rating: 10% awesome

Have an idea for a video or audio project and need help creating your script, you've come to the right place!

  • Things You'll Need

  • A writing tool, such as google docs, word, a pen and paper, or even your notes app
  • An idea
  • A storyline
  • Your characters
  • A goal

  1. Come up with an idea.

    First, you need an idea. There is a infinite amount of ways to create a story, whether you want it based on something you saw in real life, or if you want to create a completely fictious story.

  2. Organize your thoughts.

    Organize and build a plan for your story, whether it's you making a timeline, writing a simple logline, or even making a series of moodboards. This is key so you dont get mixed up while writing.

    Having your notes next to you while you write the script can help make everything cohesive.

  3. Create your script

    Whether you're working online or on paper, this is obviously the most important step. Your title page is the first part of your story, and the first step.

  4. Keep a format guide handy.

    Scripts have a unique formatting that is highly expected of any project, so keeping an accurate formatting guide open to the side is extremely handy.

  5. Set a deadline.

    In order to keep yourself on track, give yourself a realistic deadline for when you'll finish the script. Depending on how long the story is, this could be by the next week, month, or even by the next holiday.

  6. Create a rough draft.

    Your first draft of the script will be your rough draft. Now that you have the rough draft, its time to look over it and change anything that needs to be changed, like typos, dialogue that maybe doesn't make sense, or just anything that doesn't feel right. You can also have someone else review it for a fresh new set of eyes to see.

  7. Fix any issues from the rough draft/Polishing your script.

    After reviewing your draft, nows the time to polish and fix up any issues that exist. Once you feel everything is in it's right place, and also had another round of peer review, you are on your final draft.

  8. Final draft.

    Once your script is polished to perfection, you have your final draft. What you decide to do with this draft is up to you, you can keep it as a portfolio piece, create the project yourself, or even submit it to production companies to see if they would like to produce it.
